(01) The studio माटी का · maati ka · of the earth

MAATIQA is a ceramic design studio in Indore. We start with natural clay , design each form on screen , print it one layer at a time, and finish every piece by hand .

i.

Material first

Natural clay is the starting point. Its texture, behaviour and limits inform every decision — from the first test to the final fired form.

ii.

Digital craft

Each form begins as a digital model. Layer-by-layer clay printing makes it possible to explore profiles, rhythms and structures beyond conventional hand-building methods.

iii.

Finished by hand

A growing collection of vessels and sculptural forms, alongside bespoke pieces developed for homes, hospitality spaces and design-led projects.

(02) Material

It’s clay, not plastic.

Every ridge you can feel on a MAATIQA piece is a single line of earth, laid down by the printer and left visible on purpose. The surface is the record of how the object was made.

(03) ProcessEarth → object · four stages

5,000 years of coiling, reimagined.

The process takes inspiration from the traditional coiling method: a continuous line of clay built layer upon layer. Digital models guide the print; careful preparation, finishing and firing bring each object to completion.

  1. 01 — Maati

    The material

    The clay is prepared and conditioned for printing, balancing workability with enough structure to support each successive layer.

  2. 02 — Design

    The model

    Every form starts as geometry: a profile, a rhythm, a twist. The numbers are adjusted until the shape feels inevitable, then sliced into printable layers.

  3. Studio footage
    03 — Print

    The print

    A controlled extrusion process builds the form one coil at a time. Print speed, material consistency and geometry all influence the final surface.

  4. 04 — Fire

    The finish

    Pieces dry slowly, are cleaned by hand and fired in a kiln. Small irregularities stay. They’re the proof that it was made from real earth.

(06) From the studioIndore · 2026

Notes from the studio.

MAATIQA took shape in Indore through material research, experimentation and the work of building a dedicated studio. Today, that space brings together digital making, hands-on craft and an evolving collection of ceramic forms.

Journal 01 · Behind the material

Not every form survives.

Clay stays alive until it’s fired. Humidity, weight and timing all have a say, and hours of precision can fold in a single moment. The failures show us where the material wants to go.

Journal 02 · Workshops

Mitti, by hand.

Not everything begins with a printer. MAATIQA also brings people together through hands-on clay workshops — creating space to learn, shape and connect with the material.

No POP · No mould · Just mitti

Journal 03 · The space

Built from zero to one.

Research, paperwork, a bare shell and a lot of dust. The studio was built by hand, the same way everything else here is made.

“It’s not how it’s made. It’s the effort behind it.”
